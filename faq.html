Frequently Asked Questions about AR Stroke Rehabilitation

Quick answers about RAHBAR’s pose tracking, joint angle analysis, seated exercises, privacy and how to get started.

AI assessmentPrivacy first

What is RAHBAR AR rehabilitation technology?

RAHBAR uses camera-based pose estimation to track joint movements in real time and project visual Augmented Reality (AR) guidance to assist stroke survivors while performing exercises.

Can patients who cannot stand perform rehabilitation exercises?

Yes. RAHBAR explicitly supports clinician-approved seated rehabilitation exercises so that patients with limited standing ability or balance issues can participate safely.

How does RAHBAR analyze joint angles and range of motion (ROM)?

The system uses computer vision landmarks to dynamically calculate real-time joint angles, such as elbow, shoulder and knee flexibility, and compares them against therapist-configured targets.

Does the system continuously store heavy patient video files?

No. To protect patient privacy and optimize server storage, RAHBAR stores structured numerical session analytics (joint measurements, repetition counts and progress scores) instead of raw video recordings.

How do physiotherapists monitor patient progress?

Physiotherapists have access to a dedicated dashboard where they can review past session comparisons, track recovery milestones and assign customized exercise plans for each patient.

Who developed RAHBAR?

RAHBAR was developed by Aqsa Ahmed, Nayab Goher and Areeba Nadeem as a Senior Design Project at Riphah International University, supervised by Dr. Hajra Murtaza.
